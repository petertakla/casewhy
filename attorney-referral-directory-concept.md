# Attorney Referral/Directory Feature — Concept & Scope

**Status: scope and business-development research only — not yet authorized to build.** Same posture CW-39 (the escalation toolkit) started from: size it and understand it properly before committing engineering time, because this one has a real legal-compliance dimension that a normal feature doesn't.

## Where this came from

Came out of a "should CaseWhy run ads on the free tier" discussion (Sep 7). Short version of that conclusion: probably not — the audience is people anxious about a pending immigration case, exactly who predatory "notario"/fake-consultant ads target, and CaseWhy's own landing page already leans hard on trust ("your data isn't for sale," encryption, no data sales). A programmatic ad network can't be hand-picked, so it risks putting CaseWhy's name next to the exact kind of scam-adjacent ad its own messaging implicitly promises it isn't. VisaWatch (a tracked competitor) has turned "zero ads, ever" into its own trust-marketing line, which is a real signal that *not* running ads is itself worth something in this market.

The better lever was already sitting in the existing monetization-ideas list from the Sep 5 competitive research (ranked #6 there): **an attorney referral/directory feature** — a warm handoff to vetted immigration attorneys, offered at the exact moment the app already tells a user "this is outside what we can help with, talk to a lawyer." Same underlying idea as Lawfully's own directory (already tracked in the competitive-comparison artifact: "Built-in directory + reviews; sponsored listings in free tier").

## The part that makes this different from a normal feature: attorney referral fees are a regulated area, not just a product decision

This needed real research, not assumption, before scoping a build — and it changes the shape of what gets built.

**The core rule, consistent across states (ABA Model Rules, which most states' bars mirror closely):** a lawyer cannot pay a non-lawyer for referring clients, and cannot split legal fees with a non-lawyer. Rule 5.4(a): "a lawyer or law firm shall not share legal fees with a non-lawyer." Rule 7.2(b): a lawyer can't give "anything of value to a person for recommending the lawyer's services." A **per-referral or per-case-outcome payment from an attorney to CaseWhy would be exactly the arrangement this prohibits** — this rules out a commission/pay-per-referred-client model outright, not as a preference but as a real ethics violation risk for any attorney who agreed to it.

**The standard, well-precedented workaround — and it's genuinely standard, not a workaround CaseWhy would be inventing:** this is exactly how Avvo, Martindale-Nolo, LegalMatch, and Thumbtack Legal already operate. Attorneys pay a **flat advertising/listing fee** — for exposure or a bundle of leads over a period, set in advance, not contingent on whether a case actually converts or what it's worth — rather than a cut of each referral. A flat fee for marketing exposure is advertising, not fee-splitting, under the same rules above. This is the model CaseWhy's directory should be built around from day one: **attorneys pay CaseWhy a flat, recurring fee to be listed/featured, period — never a percentage or per-lead commission.**

**Separately, and worth not conflating with the fee-splitting question: some states also regulate the referral *service itself*, independent of how it's paid.** Florida (relevant directly, since CaseWhy LLC is Florida-based) has Bar Rule 4-7.22, which defines "qualifying providers" broadly enough to explicitly include "lawyer referral services, matching services, group or pooled advertising programs, **directories**, or tips or leads generators" — meaning a flat-fee attorney directory almost certainly counts, even though the fee-splitting problem is solved by the flat-fee structure. For-profit qualifying providers under that rule have to: file annual reports listing participating lawyers (minimum four lawyers from different firms to be listed), report to the Bar every July 1, disclose each referred lawyer's location when a referral happens, avoid implying Bar endorsement, and respond to Bar inquiries within 15 days. None of this is disqualifying — Avvo and Martindale-Nolo operate under equivalent rules in every state they're in — but it's real compliance overhead, not a pure code task, and **if CaseWhy refers users to attorneys in states other than Florida (likely, given the app isn't state-limited), each of those states' own referral-service rules could also apply**, not just Florida's. This is squarely in "get it looked at by a lawyer" territory, not something to guess through — and it's a good candidate to fold into the attorney-review decision already deferred elsewhere in this project (round 13's billing-terms review), rather than opening a second, separate legal-review track.

**A real way to de-risk this without waiting on that review: ship a non-monetized version first.** A curated, hand-maintained list of vetted immigration attorneys — shown as a genuine resource, nobody paying to be listed, no fee changing hands — is much closer to the links/resources hub already scoped in round 14 than to a regulated "referral service." Florida's rule, and the equivalent in most states, is aimed at *for-profit* referral arrangements; an editorial, unpaid resource list sidesteps most of that question entirely (still worth a real confirmation, not an assumption, before calling it fully clear). That gives a real Phase 1 that ships now, with the paid/flat-fee-listing version as Phase 2, explicitly gated on the same legal review as the billing terms.

## Proposed build (once authorized)

**Phase 1 — unpaid, editorial directory (no legal-compliance blocker, buildable now if authorized):**
- A hand-curated table of immigration attorneys (same "small, easy to edit" pattern as `src/lib/congress/representatives.ts` and `NEWS_SOURCES`), each entry: name, firm, state(s) licensed, practice focus, contact method, and a plain note that this is an informational list, not an endorsement or referral service.
- Vetting before any attorney is added: confirm active bar admission and good standing via that state's public attorney-lookup tool — non-negotiable, both for CaseWhy's own trust brand and because listing a lapsed or sanctioned attorney would be a real problem.
- Surfaced at the same "talk to an attorney" moments the app already has (the UPL guardrail language throughout the product, the escalation toolkit's "when this needs more than a letter" moment) — a natural extension of messaging that already exists, not a new UX pattern.
- No payment processing, no per-referral tracking, no revenue in Phase 1 — this is a trust/value-add feature first, an eventual monetization path second.

**Phase 2 — flat-fee paid listings, gated on legal review (not authorized, don't build without an explicit go-ahead post-review):**
- Attorneys pay a flat recurring fee (monthly or annual — pricing itself is a separate future decision) for a featured/upgraded listing — never a per-lead or per-case fee.
- Needs: a simple attorney-facing intake/application flow, an admin approval step (Peter reviews before anyone goes live — same "human in the loop" pattern as everything else in this trust-sensitive product), Stripe Checkout for the flat fee (infrastructure already exists from round 13, this would reuse it rather than build new billing), and the Florida Rule 4-7.22 reporting obligations (annual report, location disclosure on referral, minimum-four-lawyers-per-firm-diversity rule) built in from the start rather than retrofitted.
- Rough sizing, same "size it honestly" instruction as prior scope-only rounds: Phase 1 (editorial directory) is a small build, comparable to the links/resources hub in round 14 — a few hours. Phase 2 (paid listings + admin approval + compliance reporting) is meaningfully bigger, closer to CW-38/CW-39 scale, and shouldn't be estimated precisely until the legal review actually happens, since the review could change the required disclosures/workflow.

## Business development — how to actually get attorneys on board (not a code task, a parallel track like round 13's business prerequisites)

A directory with zero attorneys in it isn't useful to anyone, so this needs real outreach, and it's worth starting even before Phase 1 ships, so there's something to launch with rather than an empty page. A few concrete approaches, roughly in the order they're easiest to act on:

**Start with whoever helped your own family's N-400 case, if that relationship is still warm.** A real attorney who already knows CaseWhy's story firsthand is the single easiest first "yes" — highest trust, lowest-friction ask, and a genuine reference/testimonial if they're willing, which matters a lot for convincing the next ones.

**Go local before going national.** CaseWhy LLC is Florida-based and you're in the Tampa Bay area — a handful of Tampa/Orlando-area immigration attorneys as founding partners is easier to build in person (an actual meeting or phone call beats a cold email), and a local track record and testimonials make the pitch to attorneys further away much stronger later, rather than trying to cold-approach nationally on day one.

**AILA — the American Immigration Lawyers Association — is the natural professional channel.** It's the main association immigration attorneys actually belong to, has a Florida chapter with local meetings, and its members are already the exact audience that pays for Avvo/Martindale-Nolo leads today — meaning they already understand and expect this kind of arrangement, which is a much easier pitch than explaining the concept from scratch. Attending a local chapter event (or even just browsing its member directory for a first outreach list) is a realistic, concrete next step.

**Direct outreach to solo and small immigration practices, not big firms.** Solo/small-firm immigration attorneys are the actual target market for lead-gen platforms industry-wide — they don't have big marketing budgets, they're already comfortable paying flat fees for exposure (Avvo, Martindale, Thumbtack), and "a channel of people actively tracking a real, in-progress USCIS case" is a meaningfully higher-intent audience than generic search-ad traffic, which is a genuinely differentiated pitch worth making directly (LinkedIn outreach or a short, direct email with a one-page explanation of what CaseWhy is and who its users are).

**Offer the first 5-10 attorneys a free founding-partner period** (e.g., a few months free) in exchange for real feedback and a testimonial/case study — lowers the barrier to a first commitment, and gives Phase 2's eventual paid pitch real proof ("here's what founding partners said") instead of a cold ask.

**Worth keeping in mind as a complementary, not-for-revenue angle:** a connection to a pro bono or legal-aid immigration organization, for users who can't afford a private attorney, is good for trust and could be genuinely press-worthy alongside the founder story already being developed elsewhere in this project — not a revenue channel, but worth having in the mix rather than treating this purely as a monetization feature.
