I spent fifteen years as a Solution Architect. Structured problems, defined inputs, a clear path to a solution — that was the job.

Then my wife, my daughter, and I filed for naturalization within weeks of each other in 2025. My wife and I had our interviews in November. We submitted every document USCIS asked for. Then, months later, we finally got there: both of us scheduled for our Oath Ceremony — the very last step.

Then both ceremonies were cancelled. No new date since.

It turned out our cases had been caught by a January 2026 USCIS policy memo pausing naturalization decisions for applicants born in a list of about 39 countries — not because of anything specific to us, just a blanket hold. When our attorney tried to get answers, USCIS's own system classified the case as "within normal processing time," which meant there was no one to actually escalate to.

So we did what most people don't know is even an option: a formal inquiry through our congressman's office, a written follow-up to the local field office, and looking into the [USCIS Ombudsman's Case Assistance program](https://www.dhs.gov/ombudsman-case-assistance) as a next step. In June, a federal court struck down the policy that had been holding our cases — and a lot of other people's — since January. Our ceremony still got cancelled after that. We're still waiting.

What that whole stretch taught me: the hardest part of this process usually isn't the paperwork. It's not knowing *why* nothing is happening, or what you're actually allowed to do about it. [USCIS's own portal](https://egov.uscis.gov/) gives you a status. It doesn't explain anything.

That's what I've been building. CaseWhy takes your USCIS status and explains, in plain language, what it actually means and what real next steps exist — file an inquiry, contact your representative, when it's time to loop in an attorney — instead of just "processing." And because the people who need help most are often the ones who can least afford it, the directory of free legal aid, accredited representatives, and attorneys is free to use, with no ads, and will stay that way.

I'm building it in public, mid-story myself. If you're going through something similar, I'd like to hear about it — hello@casewhy.com.

— Peter, founder, CaseWhy

*This is my own family's experience, told with our permission and without case identifiers. It is not a description of how any other case will go, and nothing here is legal advice.*
