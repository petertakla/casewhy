**Short answer:** "Case Was Received" means USCIS has your application, has accepted the fee, and has assigned it a receipt number. It does not mean anyone has read it yet. For most case types, this status stays put for months, and that is normal.

## What it confirms

When you check a receipt number on [USCIS Case Status Online](https://egov.uscis.gov/), "Case Was Received" is the first real status most applications show. It corresponds to the receipt notice USCIS mails you — the Form I-797C — which USCIS describes as confirmation that a form was received, not a decision on it ([USCIS, Form I-797 types](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/form-i-797-types-and-functions)).

So three things are true at this point: your packet cleared the lockbox or service center intake, your payment went through, and your case now has the receipt number you'll use for everything from here on.

## What it doesn't confirm

It doesn't mean an officer has looked at the file. It doesn't mean the evidence you sent was sufficient. And it doesn't start any clock other than the one USCIS uses to publish its own processing-time estimates.

That last part matters, because the status will usually not change for a long time. USCIS publishes how long it took to complete 80% of cases of each type over the previous six months ([USCIS processing times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processingtimes/)). For many forms that figure is measured in months, sometimes more than a year. A case that shows "Case Was Received" for the whole of that window is not stuck — it's in line.

## What's worth doing while it sits there

Nothing in this post is advice about your specific case — it's the general process, as USCIS itself describes it.

**Keep the receipt notice.** The receipt number on it is how you check status, how you file an inquiry later, and how an attorney would find the case. If you never got a paper notice, USCIS's case status page will still recognize the number once it's in the system.

**Check the processing-time page for your form and office, once.** Write down the date the page shows. USCIS only lets you submit an "outside normal processing time" inquiry after that date has passed, so knowing it up front tells you when a silence turns into something you can act on.

**Make sure USCIS can reach you.** Requests for evidence and interview notices go to the mailing address on file. If you move, USCIS's own guidance is to update your address with them directly, not just with the post office.

## When this status *should* have changed

If the published processing time for your form has passed and the status still reads "Case Was Received," that's the point where USCIS's own tools open up: the e-Request for a case outside normal processing time, and beyond that a congressional inquiry or the CIS Ombudsman. We'll cover that path in a separate post, "When your case goes silent."
