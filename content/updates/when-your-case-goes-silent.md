**Short answer:** USCIS considers a case "outside normal processing time" only once it has been pending longer than the date shown on its own processing-times page for that form and office. Before that date, there is no formal channel to ask about a delay. After it, there are three.

## First, find your line

USCIS publishes a processing-time estimate for each form, and often for each office, based on how long it took to complete 80% of similar cases over the last six months ([USCIS processing times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processingtimes/)). The same page has a "receipt date for a case inquiry" tool: enter the date USCIS received your case, and it tells you whether you're past the point where an inquiry is accepted.

That single date is the hinge for everything below. A case that's quiet but still inside the window is, by USCIS's own definition, normal — even when it doesn't feel that way.

## Channel one: the e-Request

Once your receipt date is older than the inquiry date, you can file a "Case Outside Normal Processing Time" request through USCIS's [e-Request tool](https://egov.uscis.gov/e-request/). You'll need the receipt number and the details from your receipt notice. USCIS responds by mail or email; the responses are often brief, but the request creates a record that the case was flagged, which matters for the next two channels.

You can also call the [USCIS Contact Center](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/contact-us). The automated system handles most calls; getting to a person usually requires that the case already be outside normal processing time.

## Channel two: your member of Congress

Every U.S. Senator and Representative has caseworkers who handle constituent immigration inquiries. You sign a privacy release, they contact USCIS's congressional liaison on your behalf, and USCIS is obliged to respond to the office. It doesn't change the outcome of a case, and it doesn't jump the line — but it reliably produces a written status explanation, which is often more than an applicant can get directly.

## Channel three: the CIS Ombudsman

The [Office of the Citizenship and Immigration Services Ombudsman](https://www.dhs.gov/ombudsman-case-assistance), part of DHS but independent of USCIS, takes case-assistance requests when the normal channels haven't resolved a problem. Their own guidance asks that you try USCIS's channels first and be able to show it. Requests go through their online form (DHS Form 7001).

## What "normal" looks like from the inside

A case that sits for months past its interview, or past its published window, with every inquiry answered "within normal processing time," is not rare — it's a pattern many applicants describe, and one our founder has lived through with his own family's naturalization cases. The path above is the one that exists. It is slow, it is procedural, and it works better when each step is documented.

*This post describes USCIS's published process in general terms. It is not legal advice and cannot tell you what to do in your specific case; for that, a licensed immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative is the right person to ask.
