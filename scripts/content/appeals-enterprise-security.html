What happens
to an uploaded notice.

Designed to hold as little about your residents as the work allows.

CaseWhy Appeals is not affiliated with or endorsed by Medicare, CMS, or any health plan, and is not a law firm.

The document

  1. Uploaded. A facility photographs or uploads the Medicare notice.
  2. Read. CaseWhy takes the dates, the notice type and the denial reason.
  3. Deleted. The document itself is removed on a schedule — within 48 hours on the ordinary path, and 14 days for a case that stalled before review. A scheduled job does this, not somebody remembering.

What is kept, and what is not

  • No resident name is required. There is no resident-name field on a case. The facility's own reference is free text, and the product refuses something that looks like a person's name — in the form and in a bulk import.
  • Medicare numbers and Social Security numbers are masked before any record is written, by a redaction step that runs ahead of storage. There is deliberately no column holding the unredacted reading.
  • Sensitive fields are encrypted at rest, with the key held apart from the database.
  • Transport is encrypted throughout.

Who can see what

Access follows your organizational structure rather than a single administrator password. A facility administrator sees their building. A regional director sees their region. An executive or an analyst sees the portfolio in figures, and no individual resident is shown at that level — that is a deliberate limit, not an omission. Every access decision, including every refusal and its reason, is recorded.

Business associate agreements

Handling a notice at all makes CaseWhy a business associate under HIPAA, whatever is deleted afterwards. We are putting the agreement and the safeguards behind it in place rather than claiming none is needed. If your organization needs one executed before it can use the product, say so and you will get a straight answer about where that stands rather than a form.
