CaseWhy Appeals
for healthcare organizations

Medicare coverage denials are appealable on a two-day clock, and independent reviewers overturn roughly half of them. For an organization operating multiple buildings, the material question is not whether appeals succeed. It is how many are never filed, and whether anyone would know.

CaseWhy Appeals is not affiliated with or endorsed by Medicare, CMS, or any health plan, and is not a law firm.

The exposure

CMS reviewed 625,608 fast-track appeals of post-acute coverage terminations between May 2024 and September 2025. Independent reviewers (BFCC-QIOs) disagreed with the plan in approximately half of them. For denied admissions, the HHS Office of Inspector General reported in June 2026 that 95% of appealed denials were overturned.

Two conclusions follow for an organization operating at scale. First, an appeal not filed is, more often than not, a reversible decision left unchallenged. Second, the determining factor is administrative rather than clinical: the fast-track deadline is a call to the reviewer before noon on the day before coverage ends, and it is met or missed by whoever happens to be handling the notice that day.

At a single site that risk is visible to the administrator. Across twenty or forty sites it is visible to no one, because there has not been a report that shows it.

One screen for the whole portfolio

Every building you operate, ordered by what is closest to being missed. The question is not how many appeals are open — it is which site is about to lose one, and whose name is against it. Both are answered on the first screen rather than assembled afterwards from a report somebody has to ask for.

The same screen serves two readers who are usually sent two different reports: operations leadership reviews by region, line-of-business leadership reviews by care segment, and switching between them is one click rather than a different export.

What an organization gets that a single facility does not

  • Portfolio-level visibility of the nearest deadline. Not when a given deadline falls, but which building is closest to missing one.
  • Named accountability on every open appeal. Appeals lapse for want of an owner more often than for want of time.
  • Regional and segment views of the same data. Operations leadership reviews by region; line-of-business leadership reviews by care segment. Both are required and they are different reports.
  • A single point of access rather than separate credentials per site.

Risk and compliance posture

No business associate agreement is required. This follows from the architecture rather than from a contractual position. The notice is read, the dates and denial reason are extracted, and the file is then deleted. No protected health information is retained for an agreement to govern.

For most organizations this removes the procurement review rather than passing it. Resident names and Medicare numbers are never entered; each case carries the organization's own reference; access is granted per seat, with account owners able to view the full organization and members their own site. The Privacy Policy is the complete statement.

Commercial terms

Priced per site, per care segment. Skilled nursing, home health and hospice carry separate pricing because they are separate operations. Published per-site rates appear on each segment's page.

Skilled nursing is shown above. Home health and hospice are documented separately, and each is priced the same way: one flat monthly fee per site covering every case at any volume, with pay-as-you-go available instead.

A free period per care segment, not per organization. An organization operating in two or three segments receives a separate period for each. A single organization-wide period would oblige two evaluations to proceed on a third's timetable, and the personnel evaluating skilled nursing are generally not those evaluating home health.

No enterprise rate card is published here, because none has been established. No multi-facility organization has yet subscribed, and a stated volume discount would not reflect an actual agreement. Pricing against a specific footprint is provided on request.

Evaluating without commitment

A pilot uses the standard free period. There is no separate programme, agreement or minimum.

  • Two or three buildings within one care segment.
  • The period begins at the first letter generated, not at account creation. An account opened now and first used in several months receives its full period, so there is no incentive to begin before a genuine notice arrives.
  • Cases opened during the free period are never billed, including their letters and tracked deadlines. No appeal in progress is affected by a commercial decision.
  • The decision at the end is made per care segment. The remaining segments will not have started their own periods.

What is implemented today

Stated plainly, because an evaluation should not have to distinguish between a capability and an intention.

  • A consolidated view of every building, showing open appeals, the next deadline at each site, and whether the case has an assigned owner.
  • Navigation from organization to care segment to building to case. The building-level view is the interface a single facility already uses; consolidation is added above it rather than as a second system.
  • Combined filtering by care segment and region, with regions defined by the organization rather than inferred from state.
  • An assigned owner on every case, set at creation and reassignable by an account owner.
  • Deadlines calculated from the notice by deterministic rule, incorporating a holiday and weekend schedule. No language model participates in producing a date.

Appeal outcome reporting is not yet available. The data model supports it; no outcomes have been recorded, and it will not be presented as a capability until there is evidence behind it.
