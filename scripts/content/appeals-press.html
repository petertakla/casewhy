Press & research — CaseWhy Appeals

The story in three sentences

Peter, CaseWhy's founder, spent fifteen years as a solution architect. He built CaseWhy first to explain confusing USCIS case letters, then found the same problem in Medicare: a denial arrives with no plain explanation of what was denied, why, or by when it has to be appealed — and almost nobody appeals it, even though the odds favor doing so. CaseWhy Appeals is built to close that gap: it explains the notice, writes the appeal, and tracks both deadlines.

CaseWhy Appeals is not affiliated with or endorsed by Medicare, CMS, or any health plan, and is not a law firm.

The appeal paradox

Only about 1 in 9 Medicare Advantage prior-authorization denials gets appealed (11.5%) — but more than 8 in 10 of the appeals that are filed win (80.7%). Source: KFF, Jan. 28, 2026, analyzing 2024 CMS Medicare Advantage prior-authorization data.

Facts

  • What it is: a web app at appeals.casewhy.com that explains a Medicare denial notice in plain language with the rule cited, computes both appeal deadlines, writes the appeal letter, fills the CMS forms for that level, and tracks the case through every level up to the Medicare Appeals Council.
  • Coverage: every Medicare appeal type — Medicare Advantage, Part D, Original Medicare, premiums (IRMAA and late-enrollment penalties), enrollment, and PACE. See the full coverage map.
  • Professional seats: free, permanently, for SHIP/SHINE counselors, long-term care ombudsman programs, legal aid, and nonprofit staff. For independent advocates, care managers and attorneys: the first 3 cases free, then pay-per-case with no monthly fee, or a monthly plan at higher volume. Facilities are licensed per site, priced separately for skilled nursing, home health and hospice.
  • Data: no client identity is ever stored — a client is a label and a reference number the professional chooses.
  • Company: CaseWhy LLC, St. Petersburg, Florida, founded 2026. No outside funding.

Founder

Peter, founder, Florida. Fifteen years as a solution architect in enterprise IT; built CaseWhy to explain USCIS case letters, then built CaseWhy Appeals after learning how rarely Medicare Advantage denials get appealed — and how often they win when they are.
